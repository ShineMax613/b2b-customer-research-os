# End User vs Distributor vs Competitor Checklist

Use this checklist to identify whether a lead is likely to be an end user, distributor, wholesaler, contractor, trader, or competitor before sending sensitive information such as full price lists, detailed quotations, supplier terms, or product sources.

## 2. Possible End User Signals

The lead may be an end user if they:

* Mention their own hotel, resort, furnished apartment, store, warehouse, restaurant, hospital, project, or facility
* Ask about products for actual use
* Mention delivery city or project location
* Ask about product quality, durability, availability, samples, or delivery time
* Care about service, consistency, urgent supply, or after-sales support
* Ask for selected items rather than every product in the catalog
* Share project details, room count, branch count, or usage scenario
* Ask operational questions instead of only asking for price

## 3. Possible Distributor / Wholesaler Signals

The lead may be a distributor or wholesaler if they:

* Ask for wholesale price
* Ask for full catalog
* Ask about MOQ
* Ask about regular supply
* Ask about different models, sizes, or packaging options
* Mention resale, distribution, retail, or customers
* Compare multiple product lines
* Ask for fast-moving items
* Ask about branding, carton quantity, or stock availability
* Want to know whether you can support long-term cooperation

## 4. Possible Contractor / Project Buyer Signals

The lead may be a contractor or project buyer if they:

* Mention a project, tender, BOQ, site, consultant, or client
* Ask for technical specifications
* Ask for compliance documents, certificates, or approvals
* Ask for delivery schedule
* Ask for project quotation
* Ask for multiple categories related to one project
* Need documentation before price confirmation
* Ask about installation, warranty, or after-sales support

## 5. Possible Trader / Price Hunter Signals

The lead may be a trader or price hunter if they:

* Ask for price immediately without sharing requirement
* Ask for many product prices without quantity
* Ask for full price list before introducing company
* Refuse to share delivery city or company information
* Keep pushing for “best price” only
* Avoid answering basic qualification questions
* Compare only price and not quality, stock, documents, or delivery
* Ask for sensitive supplier information too early

## 6. Possible Competitor Signals

The lead may be a competitor if they:

* Ask for full price list immediately
* Ask for many unrelated SKUs
* Ask for detailed product sources, factory information, or cost structure
* Avoid sharing company name or buying purpose
* Use vague identity or personal profile only
* Claim large demand but provide no project, quantity, or delivery information
* Ask questions that sound more like market research than purchasing
* Try to collect product photos, specifications, and price lists without clear buying intent

## 7. Lead Classification

Choose the most likely lead type:

* End user
* Distributor
* Wholesaler
* Contractor
* Project buyer
* Trader
* Competitor
* Unknown
* Need more information

## 8. Information Sharing Recommendation

Based on the lead type, decide what to share.

### If likely end user:

Recommended to share:

* Selected product photos
* Relevant catalog pages
* Availability information
* Item-based quotation
* Delivery and service information

Avoid sharing:

* Full price list too early
* Supplier cost details
* Unnecessary product source information

### If likely distributor or wholesaler:

Recommended to share:

* Product category overview
* Fast-moving item list
* MOQ information
* Selected wholesale quotation
* Cooperation introduction
* Stock and customization options

Avoid sharing:

* Full price list without qualification
* Sensitive supplier or factory cost details
* All pricing before understanding volume and market role

### If likely contractor or project buyer:

Recommended to share:

* Company introduction
* Relevant product documents
* Compliance documents if available
* Project-based quotation after requirement confirmation
* Delivery and support capability

Avoid sharing:

* Random general price list
* Incomplete quotation without BOQ or specification
* Sensitive commercial terms before project clarity

### If likely competitor or price hunter:

Recommended to share:

* General company introduction only
* Limited product information
* Ask qualification questions first
* Request company details, quantity, and delivery city

Avoid sharing:

* Full price list
* Detailed quotation
* Supplier information
* Cost structure
* Complete catalog with sensitive pricing

## 9. Qualification Questions to Ask

Use these questions before sending quotation or price list:

1. May I know your company name?
2. Which items are you interested in?
3. What quantity do you need?
4. Which city or country should we deliver to?
5. Are you buying for your own project, hotel, store, or for resale?
6. Do you need regular stock items or customized branding?
7. Is this for immediate purchase, project planning, or price comparison?

## 10. Suggested Message

Hi [Name], thank you for your inquiry.

To recommend the right products and prepare a suitable offer, may I know whether you are buying for your own project / hotel / store, or for resale and distribution?

Also, please share the items, quantity, and delivery city, so we can check availability and quote correctly.

## 11. Final Decision

Choose one:

* Treat as end user
* Treat as distributor / wholesaler
* Treat as contractor / project buyer
* Treat as trader
* Treat as possible competitor
* Ask more questions first
* Share limited information only
* Send selected quotation
* Do not send full price list yet
* Stop following
