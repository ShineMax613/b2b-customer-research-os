# Customer Background Research Prompt

Use this prompt to analyze a potential B2B customer before sending a quotation, catalog, full price list, or follow-up message.

This prompt is designed for B2B exporters, manufacturers, and international sales teams.

## Prompt

Act as a B2B export sales research analyst.

Your task is to analyze a potential customer based on the information I provide.

Please help me determine:

1. Whether this company appears to be real and relevant
2. What type of company it is
3. Whether the contact may be an end user, distributor, wholesaler, contractor, trader, or competitor
4. Whether this lead has real buying intent
5. What information is still missing
6. What risks or red flags I should notice
7. Whether I should send a catalog, quotation, full price list, or ask more questions first
8. What follow-up message I should send next

## Analysis Requirements

Please analyze the lead using the following structure:

### 1. Company Background Summary

Summarize what this company appears to do based on the information provided.

### 2. Company Type

Classify the company as one or more of the following:

* End user
* Distributor
* Wholesaler
* Retailer
* Trader
* Contractor
* Project company
* Hotel / resort / furnished apartment
* Manufacturer
* Competitor
* Unknown

Explain your reasoning.

### 3. Contact Role Analysis

Analyze whether the contact person appears to be:

* Owner / founder
* General manager
* Procurement manager
* Purchasing manager
* Operations manager
* Project manager
* Salesperson
* Distributor buyer
* Wholesaler
* Contractor
* Competitor researcher
* Unknown

Estimate their decision power:

* High
* Medium
* Low
* Unknown

Explain your reasoning.

### 4. Buyer Intent Analysis

Classify the buying intent as:

* High
* Medium
* Low
* Unknown

Consider:

* Product specificity
* Quantity clarity
* Delivery city
* Timeline
* Company information
* Type of questions asked
* Willingness to answer qualification questions

### 5. Lead Quality Score

Give a score from 0 to 100.

Use this scoring logic:

* Company information provided: 15 points
* Clear product requirement: 20 points
* Quantity mentioned: 20 points
* Delivery city mentioned: 10 points
* Contact role is relevant: 15 points
* Urgency or timeline mentioned: 10 points
* Communication quality is professional: 10 points

Then classify the lead as:

* 80–100: Hot lead
* 60–79: Qualified lead
* 40–59: Nurture lead
* 20–39: Low-priority lead
* 0–19: Unqualified lead

### 6. Red Flags

List any possible red flags, such as:

* No company name
* No clear role
* No quantity
* No delivery city
* Only asks for price
* Asks for full price list immediately
* Refuses to share basic information
* Looks like competitor price checking
* Profile does not match claimed business
* Communication is vague or inconsistent

### 7. Missing Information

List the most important missing information I should ask for before quoting.

### 8. Recommended Next Step

Choose one or more:

* Ask for company details
* Ask for product details
* Ask for quantity
* Ask for delivery city
* Ask whether they buy for own use or resale
* Send selected catalog
* Send selected product photos
* Send item-based quotation
* Do not send full price list yet
* Move to WhatsApp
* Add to nurture list
* Stop following

### 9. Suggested Follow-up Message

Write a professional WhatsApp or LinkedIn message I can send next.

The message should be:

* Polite
* Direct
* Not too long
* Suitable for B2B export sales
* Trust-building
* Not too aggressive
* Focused on getting the missing information needed for a proper quotation

### 10. Final Recommendation

Give a clear final recommendation:

* Quote now
* Ask more questions first
* Send catalog only
* Send limited information
* Nurture
* Do not continue

Also explain why.

## Important Rules

Do not make unsupported claims.

If information is missing, say “unknown” instead of guessing.

Do not assume someone is a competitor without evidence.

Do not judge a buyer only because they ask for price first. In many markets, buyers may start with price, but qualification questions are still needed.

Focus on practical B2B sales decisions.
